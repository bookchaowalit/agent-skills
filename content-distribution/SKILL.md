# Content Distribution

Turn useful expertise into channel-appropriate content that earns attention and produces a measurable next step.

## Distribution workflow

1. Define the audience, problem, offer, desired action, voice, proof available, channel, and publication status.
2. Start from evidence: repository-owned knowledge, verified delivery results, user-provided material, or clearly attributed sources. Separate facts, opinions, hypotheses, and examples.
3. Create one strong source asset, then derive variants for the selected channels. Preserve the central claim while adapting length, format, tone, call to action, and accessibility.
4. For search content, match search intent, use a clear heading structure, write useful metadata, add relevant internal links, and avoid keyword stuffing or fabricated expertise.
5. For social or email, lead with the reader's problem, give one concrete insight, use a specific low-friction call to action, and make the next step easy to track.
6. Run a pre-publication review for factual accuracy, permissions, privacy, client confidentiality, legal or regulated claims, brand voice, links, accessibility, and formatting.
7. Keep drafts separate from approved, scheduled, and published content. Publish or send only with explicit authorization and the correct account or workspace.
8. Measure impressions, reach, click-through, saves, replies, qualified leads, conversion, and revenue. Record the hypothesis and use results to choose the next experiment.

## Decision rules

- No proof for a result → make the claim narrower or label it as a hypothesis.
- Client or personal information → anonymize, obtain permission, or omit it.
- One asset across many channels → preserve the idea, not the exact wording.
- External source → attribute it and verify the link and date before publishing.
- Unclear audience or CTA → resolve that before producing volume.
- External publication or message → leave it in draft until approved; never report it as sent or published without evidence.

## Handoff

Deliver the source asset, channel variants, approval checklist, scheduling state, source notes, tracking links or naming, and the next measurement date.
